About the Centre

Bridging academic theory and grassroots practice.

Across 2025–2026 the Centre worked to close the gap between academic theory and grassroots community practice. Guided by Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) frameworks and sustainable development, its programmes were designed to foster ecological awareness and social responsibility together rather than separately.

Central to that work has been moving students from passive classroom instruction to active, field-based engagement. By championing hands-on practice, CESS has worked to make its student body catalysts for social change rather than recipients of knowledge.

The activities of the Centre are aligned with the broader institutional commitment of CHRIST (Deemed to be University) towards academic excellence, social responsibility, sustainability and community transformation.

Vision
To be a global leader in fostering resilient, inclusive societies through innovative ecological stewardship and transformative social action research, recognized as a centre of excellence in sustainability science and practice.
Mission
To design, deliver, and evaluate evidence-based programmes and research that promote ecological justice, public health, social equity, and sustainable community development through a “glocal” (global-local) approach.

Programmes · 2025–2026

Nine programmes across three strategic tiers.

To keep its approach multidimensional, the Centre organises its work into three tiers — direct community intervention, campus-wide engagement, and academic masterclasses that bring global research alongside indigenous knowledge.

TIER 01

Community Outreach & Grassroots Interventions

Programmes directly engaging vulnerable, marginalised or external community populations to drive behavioural change.

Empowering Healthy Ageing

192Beneficiaries
Venues
LR Nagar, Tilak Nagar, and CHRIST University (administrative staff)
Focus
Geriatric care, preventive healthcare, SDG 3
Partner
St. John's Geriatric Centre

What we learned. Theoretical health advice gave way to practical, demonstrative learning — participants benefited most from live physiotherapy and aerobic demonstrations. Sustained community health requires intersectional intervention: nutritional literacy and emotional well-being alongside physical exercise.

Nasha Mukt Bharat (NMBA) Deaddiction Awareness Programme

103Beneficiaries
Venue
St. Patrick's School, Bengaluru
Focus
Substance abuse prevention, peer pressure management
Audience
Students and staff

What we learned. Traditional lecture formats proved insufficient for behavioural change. A role-play enacted by MSW students carried the familial toll of substance abuse far more effectively, establishing peer-to-peer interactive engagement as the method that works in school settings. The programme closed with a unified NMBA pledge.

TIER 02

Campus Awareness & Student Engagement Initiatives

Interactive, university-wide events designed to foster empathy, inclusivity and personal accountability among the student body and staff.

Promoting Healthy Ageing

Date
5 January 2026
Focus
Occupational wellness, active ageing, SDG 3

St. John's Geriatric Centre (SJGC) — A Ray of Hope

What we learned. A staff wellness initiative giving administrative staff insight into maintaining physical and mental well-being as they age. The takeaway was the need for proactive health intervention within the workplace — institutional sustainability begins with the holistic wellness of an institution's own workforce.

“Second Chance” — Promoting Holistic Well-being

SDG 3, SDG 10 and SDG 11

What we learned. Gamifying sustainability turned global goals into personal, actionable responsibility for young people. The sale of unused accessories promoted responsible consumption, linking personal lifestyle choices directly to ecological sustainability.

Beyond Vision: Understanding Corneal Blindness & Blind Walk

100Beneficiaries
Venue
Central Block to Block 2, CHRIST University
Focus
Empathy-building, cornea donation awareness
Partner
Sumanahalli Society

What we learned. A paradigm shift from sympathy to empathy. Testimony from a visually impaired student established that financial charity is often less empowering than actionable medical support, and the experiential Blind Walk — navigating the campus without sight — produced a surge of students pledging to donate their corneas.

The way forward

Sustainable societies are built through knowledge, collaboration, compassion and responsible action.
